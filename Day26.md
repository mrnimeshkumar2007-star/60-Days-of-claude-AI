# Day 26 - Build a Prior Authorization Workflow Simulator

### **Objective**

To create an interactive, gamified simulator that helps users understand the U.S. healthcare Prior Authorization (PA) process by visualizing the workflow between the Patient, Provider, and Payer. The simulator aims to improve learning through hands-on interaction, decision-making, and real-world scenarios.

---

## **Learning Objectives**

After using this simulator, learners should be able to:

* Understand the complete Prior Authorization workflow.
* Identify the roles of the Patient, Provider, and Payer.
* Explain the importance of medical necessity documentation.
* Recognize the documents required for PA submission.
* Understand different payer review outcomes (Approval, Pend, Denial).
* Learn when Appeals and Peer-to-Peer Reviews are appropriate.
* Appreciate how delays impact patient care and operational efficiency.
* Improve knowledge through interactive, scenario-based learning.

---

## **Key Learnings**

* Prior Authorization is a collaborative process involving multiple stakeholders.
* Complete and accurate documentation improves approval rates.
* Medical necessity is the foundation of every PA decision.
* Missing information often results in pended requests.
* Denials can frequently be challenged through appeals or Peer-to-Peer reviews.
* Efficient workflows reduce turnaround time and improve patient outcomes.
* Interactive simulations enhance engagement and retention compared to static learning materials.

## **Questions & Answers**

### **Q1. What is Prior Authorization?**

**Answer:**
Prior Authorization is a utilization management process where healthcare providers obtain approval from an insurance payer before certain medical services, procedures, or medications are covered.

---

### **Q2. Why is Prior Authorization required?**

**Answer:**
To ensure medical necessity, promote evidence-based care, manage healthcare costs, and prevent unnecessary treatments.

---

### **Q3. Who are the primary stakeholders?**

**Answer:**

* Patient
* Provider
* Payer (Insurance Company)

---

### **Q4. What is Medical Necessity?**

**Answer:**
Medical necessity means a healthcare service is appropriate, clinically justified, and supported by accepted medical guidelines.

---

### **Q5. What documents are commonly required?**

**Answer:**

* Clinical notes
* Diagnosis codes (ICD-10)
* Procedure codes (CPT/HCPCS)
* Imaging reports
* Laboratory results
* Medication history
* Treatment plans
* Supporting physician documentation

---

### **Q6. What happens after submission?**

**Answer:**
The payer reviews the request and issues one of several outcomes:

* Approval
* Pend (request for additional information)
* Denial

---

### **Q7. What is a Pend?**

**Answer:**
A pend occurs when the payer needs additional documentation or clarification before making a final decision.

---

### **Q8. What is an Appeal?**

**Answer:**
An appeal is a formal request asking the payer to reconsider a denied authorization based on additional evidence or corrected information.

---

### **Q9. What is a Peer-to-Peer Review?**

**Answer:**
A Peer-to-Peer Review is a discussion between the treating provider and the payer's medical reviewer to explain the clinical rationale for the requested service.

---

### **Q10. How does efficiency impact the workflow?**

**Answer:**
Efficient documentation, timely submissions, and prompt responses reduce delays, improve approval rates, and enhance patient satisfaction.
