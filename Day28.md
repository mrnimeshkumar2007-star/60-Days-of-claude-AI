# Day 28 - Build a Hospital Admission Readiness Simulator

## Objective

The objective of the **Hospital Admission Readiness Simulator** is to provide an interactive learning experience that helps users understand the administrative and operational processes involved in preparing a patient for hospital admission. The simulator demonstrates how admission coordinators, physicians, case managers, utilization review teams, and nursing staff collaborate to ensure patients meet clinical, insurance, and documentation requirements before admission.

---

# Learn About

This simulator helps learners understand:

* Hospital admission workflow
* Roles and responsibilities of an Admission Coordinator
* Prior Authorization (PA) process
* Insurance verification
* Clinical documentation requirements
* Physician orders and patient consent
* Bed management and patient placement
* Observation vs. Inpatient admission
* CMS 2-Midnight Rule
* Medical necessity concepts
* InterQual and Milliman criteria (overview)
* Care coordination among multidisciplinary teams
* Utilization Review (UR) processes
* Admission readiness scoring
* Operational risk management
* Healthcare governance metrics

---

# Key Learnings

* Hospital admission requires coordination across multiple departments before a patient can be admitted.
* Prior Authorization approval can directly affect admission readiness and timing.
* Accurate clinical documentation supports medical necessity and reimbursement.
* Insurance verification helps confirm eligibility and coverage before admission.
* Physician orders and informed consent are essential components of a complete admission process.
* Observation status differs from inpatient admission in terms of billing, cost-sharing, and Medicare requirements.
* Patients with conditions such as Acute Myocardial Infarction (AMI) and Congestive Heart Failure (CHF) often require comprehensive documentation to support medical necessity reviews.
* Utilization Review teams help identify potential denial risks and promote appropriate use of healthcare resources.
* Monitoring operational metrics such as denial rates, appeal rates, and turnaround times supports continuous quality improvement.
* Interactive simulations make complex healthcare workflows easier to understand and apply.

# Questions & Answers

### 1. What is Hospital Admission Readiness?

**Answer:**
Hospital Admission Readiness is the process of ensuring that all clinical, administrative, insurance, and operational requirements are completed before a patient is formally admitted to the hospital.

---

### 2. Who is responsible for coordinating admission readiness?

**Answer:**
Admission Coordinators work closely with physicians, nurses, case managers, utilization review staff, registration teams, and insurance representatives to prepare patients for admission.

---

### 3. Why is Prior Authorization important?

**Answer:**
Some healthcare services require insurance approval before they are covered. Completing Prior Authorization when required helps reduce delays and supports reimbursement.

---

### 4. Why is insurance verification necessary?

**Answer:**
Insurance verification confirms patient eligibility, benefits, and coverage requirements, helping prevent billing issues and unexpected costs.

---

### 5. What is clinical documentation?

**Answer:**
Clinical documentation includes physician notes, diagnoses, treatment plans, laboratory results, imaging reports, and other medical records that support patient care and medical necessity.

---

### 6. What is the CMS 2-Midnight Rule?

**Answer:**
The CMS 2-Midnight Rule provides guidance on when Medicare patients are generally appropriate for inpatient admission versus observation status. Observation and inpatient stays may have different billing, cost-sharing, and post-acute care implications, and eligible Medicare patients in observation status require a Medicare Outpatient Observation Notice (MOON).

---

### 7. What are InterQual and Milliman criteria?

**Answer:**
InterQual and Milliman (MCG) are widely used clinical decision support tools that assist healthcare organizations in evaluating medical necessity and determining the appropriate level of care. Their use depends on organizational policies and payer requirements.

---

### 8. What does Utilization Review (UR) do?

**Answer:**
Utilization Review evaluates whether healthcare services meet medical necessity and payer requirements, identifies potential denial risks, and supports appropriate use of healthcare resources.

---

### 9. What risks can affect admission readiness?

**Answer:**
Common risks include:

* Incomplete clinical documentation
* Insurance verification issues
* Bed availability constraints
* Missing physician orders
* Incomplete patient consent
* Medical necessity concerns

---

### 10. What happens when admission readiness is high?

**Answer:**
When all required administrative and clinical steps are complete, the patient is considered ready for admission, supporting a smoother care transition and reducing the likelihood of delays or denials.
