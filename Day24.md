# Day 24- Build Your Startup Business Strategy Report
### Objective

To validate whether enterprises are willing to adopt and pay for an AI-powered platform that automates Scope 3 carbon accounting, supplier emissions data collection, and compliance reporting.

---

### Key Learnings

#### 1. The Problem Is Real and Urgent

* Scope 3 reporting remains highly manual.
* Sustainability teams rely on spreadsheets, emails, and fragmented systems.
* Regulatory requirements such as CSRD are increasing pressure on enterprises.

#### 2. Buyers Are Clearly Identified

* Chief Sustainability Officers (CSOs)
* ESG Directors
* Heads of Supply Chain

These stakeholders have both budget influence and accountability for reporting outcomes.

#### 3. Automation Is More Valuable Than Analytics

Customers consistently expressed stronger interest in:

* Supplier engagement automation
* Data collection workflows
* Reporting accuracy

than advanced AI forecasting or benchmarking.

#### 4. Compliance Drives Purchasing Decisions

The primary buying motivation is:

* Regulatory compliance
* Audit readiness
* Investor expectations

rather than sustainability branding alone.

#### 5. Early Demand Signals Exist

* 30+ customer interviews completed
* 25-company waitlist established
* Pilot successfully executed

This indicates interest, but not yet proven willingness to pay.

---

### Key Questions

#### Question 1

**Do companies experience significant pain around Scope 3 data collection?**

**Answer:** Yes.

Interviews and pilot feedback consistently identified supplier emissions collection as one of the most time-consuming sustainability processes.

---

#### Question 2

**Who is the buyer?**

**Answer:** CSOs, ESG Directors, and Supply Chain leaders.

These roles own compliance, emissions reporting, and sustainability targets.

---

#### Question 3

**What outcome do customers value most?**

**Answer:** Reduced reporting effort and improved compliance readiness.

Customers care more about operational efficiency and risk reduction than carbon analytics.

---

#### Question 4

**Will customers evaluate a solution that automates supplier engagement?**

**Answer:** Yes.

The beta waitlist and pilot participation indicate strong interest in evaluating solutions.

---

#### Question 5

**Will customers pay €15K–€75K annually?**

**Answer:** Not yet validated.

This remains the most important business risk and requires paid pilot conversion.

# Executive Summary

This startup addresses a real and growing enterprise problem: Scope 3 carbon accounting across supply chains.

Unlike many climate-tech startups that depend on behavioral change or uncertain carbon markets, this business is tied directly to regulatory compliance, investor pressure, and board-level reporting requirements.

The strongest signal is not the technology—it is the combination of:

* 30+ customer interviews
* Completed pilot
* 25-company waitlist
* Identified budget owners
* Clear buying triggers

The biggest challenge is not product development.

The challenge is enterprise distribution.

If the company can consistently acquire customers for less than one year's contract value and achieve strong supplier participation rates, the business can become a highly sustainable B2B SaaS company.

Current evidence suggests meaningful demand exists.

The remaining question is willingness to pay and repeatable customer acquisition.

### Initial Verdict

**🟡 VALIDATE**

Strong market opportunity and good validation.

Need proof of:

* 3–5 paid pilots
* Repeatable acquisition channel
* Sustainable supplier engagement

before becoming fully investable.

# Sustainability Verdict

This startup has substantially stronger fundamentals than the average early-stage SaaS company because the pain is regulatory, budgeted, and tied to executive accountability. The combination of a defined buyer, high annual contract values, and existing validation creates a realistic path to a multi-million-euro recurring revenue business.

The business is not yet proven. The primary risk is commercial execution rather than product-market demand. If the company converts 3–5 paid pilots into annual contracts and demonstrates measurable ROI with supplier participation above 60%, it can evolve from a promising concept into a sustainable and potentially venture-scale enterprise software company. **Current verdict: 🟡 Validate, with a credible path to 🟢 Investable within the next 6–12 months.**

### Conclusion

The startup has validated that a significant and growing problem exists in enterprise Scope 3 emissions reporting. Regulatory pressure, identified budget owners, and strong early customer interest create favorable conditions for a sustainable B2B SaaS business.

However, the business has not yet validated its most critical assumption: **whether enterprises will consistently pay €15K–€75K per year for the solution.** The next milestone should focus on converting pilot customers into paid contracts and proving measurable ROI. Successfully securing 3–5 paid pilots would provide strong evidence of product-market fit and move the startup toward an investable and scalable business model.
